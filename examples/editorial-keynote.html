A deck should read
like a page,

Editorial layouts put the argument first: one idea per page, generous margins, and type that carries the structure instead of borders doing it.

01 — The argument

Most decks fail for a structural reason, not a visual one: they answer “what do we have?” when the room asked “what should we do?”

This page is a single argument. The heading states it, the paragraph defends it, and nothing else competes. When a page needs three columns, that is evidence that it is really three pages.

One idea

Per page

If you cannot say the page in one sentence, the page is not finished.

Evidence

Next to the claim

Numbers belong beside the sentence they support, not collected in an appendix.

Margin

Does the work

White space is the cheapest way to signal that a page has been edited.

02 — Working definitions

Four terms this deck uses precisely.

Page job
What the reader must be able to do after seeing this page. Written before the layout.
Evidence
The smallest number or quote that makes the claim checkable.
Folio
Page number and running head. Editing, not decoration.
Bleed
An image or field that leaves the page. Used once per section at most.
